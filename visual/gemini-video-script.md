# ENGRAM — Gemini Video Script

## Production brief

Create a cinematic four-minute product video for ENGRAM. Divide the video into six exact 40-second segments. The tone is dramatic, intelligent, restrained, and credible: a scientific investigation under pressure, not a generic AI advertisement.

Target viewers:

- Researchers and scientists.
- Forensic and incident-response analysts.
- Safety, reliability, and compliance engineers.
- Teams working with high-stakes datasets, models, and automated decisions.

Visual language:

- Dark evidence laboratory, warm factual reality versus violet hypothetical space.
- Precise camera movement, slow push-ins, sharp interface highlights.
- Use the real ENGRAM screenshots and UI where possible.
- Show one important visual at a time.
- Keep all interface text large enough to read at 720p.
- Use subtitles for the full narration.
- Do not invent scientific results, customer logos, benchmark numbers, or production claims.

Core sentence:

> ENGRAM turns a memory from something you can read into something you can investigate.

Important truth labels:

- The investigation field is synthetic demo data.
- The adapter and investigation UI are real in the demo.
- The counterfactual is sealed and was never executed.
- A visual card move changes layout only; it does not change memory state.

## Segment 1 — 00:00–00:40 — The moment trust breaks

### Visual direction

Open on a quiet research environment: notebooks, instrument readouts, a screen with a published result, then a subtle anomaly. Cut to the dual evidence image: the factual world on one side, a violet hypothetical world on the other. Keep the ENGRAM mark small and late.

### Voiceover

> A researcher opens the result that will guide the next experiment. The number looks reasonable. The conclusion sounds familiar. Then one question arrives: what evidence produced it? Not what the system remembers. Not what the interface displays. What actually happened before this answer appeared? When trust breaks, a plausible result is no longer enough. You need a path back to the source.

### On-screen text

`When trust breaks, where do you look?`

## Segment 2 — 00:40–01:20 — Meet ENGRAM

### Visual direction

Reveal the ENGRAM Canvas screenshot. Start with the whole workspace, then focus on the central graph. Show the question header and the investigation rail. Use a clean lower-third: `Evidence investigation surface`.

### Voiceover

> ENGRAM is an evidence investigation surface for memories, decisions, and derived results. If you know Obsidian, imagine a knowledge canvas where every connection has to explain its provenance. But this is not a personal notes app. Here, a relationship can be direct, derived, unattested, or merely possible. The distinction is part of the evidence. The question at the top is simple: can this memory still be trusted?

### On-screen text

`ENGRAM — Investigate memory provenance`

## Segment 3 — 01:20–02:00 — Find the compromised memory

### Visual direction

Use the real canvas screenshot. Move through the guided steps: Observe, Trace, Understand. Highlight the suspicious memory card, then select it. Keep the rest of the graph dimmed. Zoom into the custody label and the exact memory identifier.

### Voiceover

> The investigation starts with a concrete case: a synthetic field contains a memory marked as taint-flagged. The investigator does not have to search a wall of logs. ENGRAM guides the first question, isolates the suspicious node, and keeps the surrounding context visible. Selection does not rewrite the record. It only changes what we are examining. Every card is a doorway back to the underlying memory, its status, and its relationships.

### On-screen text

`Observe → Trace → Understand`

## Segment 4 — 02:00–02:40 — Follow the evidence

### Visual direction

Show the provenance graph screenshot and then a close-up of the inspector. Highlight one typed edge at a time. Reveal custody entries, content hash, confidence, field state, and impact seal. Avoid showing the whole inspector while narrating every field.

### Voiceover

> Now the graph becomes a chain of reasoning. A direct receipt is not the same as a derived memory. A derived result without an attestation is not the same as a proven dependency. The inspector exposes the custody history, hashes, confidence, field state, and impact seal beside the narrative. This is the difference between a graph that looks connected and a graph that can explain why it is connected.

### On-screen text

`Direct. Derived. Unattested. Possible.`

## Segment 5 — 02:40–03:20 — Ask the counterfactual

### Visual direction

Show the Compare step. Split the screen into `FACTUAL WORLD` and `COUNTERFACTUAL WORLD`. Use the violet treatment only for the hypothetical. Display the label `sealed hypothetical — never executed` before revealing the result. Show the delta receipt and query hash, not invented metrics.

### Voiceover

> Researchers do not only ask what happened. They ask what would have happened if one dependency had been excluded or contained. ENGRAM answers that question inside a boundary. The recorded baseline stays authoritative. The hypothetical is shown beside it, sealed and explicitly marked as never executed. It is not a second history. It is a bounded comparison over the seeded query, with a receipt that lets the result be checked later.

### On-screen text

`Recorded baseline`  |  `Sealed hypothetical — never executed`

## Segment 6 — 03:20–04:00 — Preserve the answer

### Visual direction

Return to the dual evidence image, then show preview-before-commit for containment and the evidence bundle export. End on the ENGRAM logo over a quiet dark field. Leave the final sentence on screen for at least four seconds.

### Voiceover

> When the investigation is complete, ENGRAM does not ask you to trust the presentation. It gives you the evidence path: the memory, the custody chain, the typed relationships, the bounded comparison, and the exported bundle. Containment is previewed before authorization and commit. The demo field is synthetic, and the limits are visible. That is the point. In science, forensics, and safety-critical work, confidence is not a mood. It is a claim with a chain behind it.

### Closing line

> ENGRAM: turn memory into evidence you can investigate.

### Final on-screen text

`From plausible answers to verifiable provenance.`

## Gemini generation instructions

Use the attached ENGRAM visual references as continuity references. Preserve the warm factual world and violet hypothetical world as distinct visual languages. Do not add futuristic holograms over the actual interface. Do not replace the product UI with invented dashboards. Keep the narration synchronized with the single region of the screen being discussed.

Use restrained cinematic sound design: low room tone, a soft pulse during discovery, a precise tonal shift when the suspicious memory is selected, and silence under the final line. Music must never compete with the voice.

The result should feel like the opening of a scientific investigation: precise, tense, visually memorable, and honest about what is demonstrated versus synthetic or hypothetical.
